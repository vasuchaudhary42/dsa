How linked lists really works
The three-pointer discipline
Every rewiring follows the same order: save the next node, change one pointer, then advance. Changing cur.next before saving it loses the rest of the list, and there is no way back. Drawing the before and after state of one step is faster than debugging it.
Why a dummy head
Operations that might change the head (removal, merging, insertion at the front) usually need a special case. A fake node in front of the head turns the head into an ordinary node; return dummy.next at the end.
Two speeds, one list
A pointer moving twice as fast is at the end when the slow one is at the middle; a pointer started n nodes ahead finds the n-th from the end in one pass. In a cycle, the fast pointer gains one node per step, so it must meet the slow one; and the head is exactly as far from the cycle entry as the meeting point is, which is why restarting one pointer finds the entry.
